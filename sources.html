Data Sources & Attribution

Last updated: 16 November 2026

Every data source has its own coverage, update schedule, licence and reliability limitations. External links open the original publisher's site.

Forecasts and location search

Open-Meteo provides forecast, hourly and daily weather data and location geocoding for non-commercial use. Open-Meteo data is attributed under CC BY 4.0; forecasts are model-based estimates, not official BOM observations. The model-valid timestamp is not an observation time or the time the provider last regenerated the forecast. SkyPulse AU separately shows the last successful API retrieval and whether a model step is recent, delayed or stale. If a request fails, a previously fetched forecast may be displayed only with an explicit cached or failed-update label; an offline cached snapshot is location-specific and expires after two hours. The six-hour outlook sums hourly modelled precipitation, and the rain-chance figure is the highest hourly probability rather than a six-hour chance of precipitation. Null fields show as unavailable, never as zero or clear skies. For authoritative warnings, consult the Bureau of Meteorology.

Radar

RainViewer provides historical rainfall radar frames via its publicly documented API for personal/educational/small-scale community use. Radar images are not guaranteed available, may be delayed, and are not forecasts. Under its 2026 free API, the service exposes approximately two hours of historical frames at 10-minute intervals. SkyPulse AU checks the metadata every five minutes when the page is active, but this does not change source frame frequency. Displayed times are the provider's frame generation timestamps in Sydney local time (AEST/AEDT); an individual scan may be older. An explicit stale or delayed label indicates recent imagery cannot be verified. The free API is limited to Universal Blue imagery and maximum native zoom 7. It does not supply nowcast radar frames. For five-minute imagery, open the original Bureau of Meteorology radar or Weatherzone Layers, which reports a five-minute Australian mosaic. These are external viewers; SkyPulse does not host or scrape their imagery. BOM copyright rules restrict public redistribution and may require a separate data licence. Potential licensed in-app sources include The Weather Company's Australia-covering five-minute radar API; it has not been connected. Review the radar source comparison and integration prerequisites.

Map appearance and source accuracy

SkyPulse AU uses original-colour OpenStreetMap map tiles. The independent incident and warning information popups match the selected SkyPulse AU dark or light interface theme, but the underlying geographic imagery and official warning/radar layers are not modified. This helps keep source colours identifiable and avoids implying extra map accuracy.

Map tiles

© OpenStreetMap contributors provides map geographic data; tiles are served by OSM infrastructure under the applicable tile usage policy.

NSW Rural Fire Service

NSW RFS Current Incidents GeoJSON is used to display NSW fire incident locations. The data is published through Data.NSW under Creative Commons Attribution; incident data is reportedly updated around every 30 minutes at source. SkyPulse AU has additional scheduled publication delay. All decisions should be based on NSW RFS guidance.

NSW RFS Incident Detail Fields

The NSW RFS public Current Incidents GeoJSON feed supplies fire/incident name, alert category and published time, plus an HTML-formatted description containing, when provided, location, council, control status, incident type, fire indicator, size in hectares, responsible agency and an incident-updated timestamp. SkyPulse extracts only recognised plain-text fields, retains the original source attribution, and displays them in incident cards and map popups. Fields may be missing or delayed and are never inferred. The RFS states incident details are generally updated every 30 minutes and spatial geometry can have a different update time. Always follow current NSW RFS and Hazards Near Me information.

Fire Warning Areas and Incident Extents

The fire map displays official NSW RFS CAP warning polygons separately from incident footprint polygons in the RFS GeoJSON feed. A published warning polygon shows the area for which the warning applies, not the current burnt area. A published incident-area outline may depict an approximate extent, not a current flame front, evacuation order or confirmed safe boundary. These two datasets can be published or updated at different times. CAP severity must not be confused with the Australian Warning System levels Advice, Watch and Act, and Emergency Warning; when no level is identifiable it is labelled unspecified.

Location and radius overlap calculations are approximate geometry checks, not official address-based emergency advice. Missing/unmapped incidents, inaccurate coordinates, unreported warning polygons, stale feeds and ongoing fire movement can all affect accuracy. An unmatched location is not an all-clear. The app labels feed outages or stale snapshots as unverifiable rather than pretending to show current coverage. Always use NSW RFS and Hazards Near Me for current instructions.

NSW RFS Fire Danger Ratings & Total Fire Bans

NSW RFS Fire Danger XML feed provides district fire danger ratings and Total Fire Ban indicators for today and tomorrow. Attribution: © NSW Rural Fire Service. Data.NSW lists the dataset under Creative Commons Attribution. The map from a location to its fire weather district may need manual selection. A feed retrieval timestamp is not an official issue time. Always consult NSW RFS current fire danger information before making safety decisions.

Official Weather Station Observations

BOM Cessnock Airport AWS publishes measured weather conditions such as temperature, wind, dew point, pressure and rainfall since 9 am. SkyPulse AU links to these observations and does not copy them into its dashboard. Open-Meteo current conditions are model-derived and should not be interpreted as Cessnock Airport measurements.

Satellite Imagery

The dashboard links to the Bureau of Meteorology Satellite Viewer. Any artwork shown in the SkyPulse satellite card is illustrative and is not actual or live satellite data.

Official RFS CAP Warnings & Warning Areas

NSW RFS warning feeds publish alerts in an EDXL/CAP structure. Where the alert contains valid CAP polygons, SkyPulse AU can display those areas. Some alerts have no usable polygons and cannot be automatically matched to your watch zone. Attribution: © State of New South Wales (NSW Rural Fire Service). For current information go to www.rfs.nsw.gov.au.

WMO-Indexed Bureau Publications

The World Meteorological Organization's register points to a public feed of Bureau-issued CAP publications. SkyPulse AU lists recent NSW/ACT-related title matches as supplementary references, not confirmed currently active warnings or location-filtered emergency notices. The official BOM warning service remains authoritative.

Weather warnings

Australian Bureau of Meteorology RSS is intended to provide statewide NSW/ACT warning titles with direct links to full official notices, but the current scheduled importer receives HTTP 403 and the data is labelled unavailable. SkyPulse AU does not claim that there are no active warnings when the feed cannot be accessed. This service is subject to BOM's personal and non-commercial RSS terms, and should never be used as the sole source during warning events.

NSW SES, official alerts, road incidents and news

The app links directly to NSW SES HazardWatch, Hazards Near Me NSW, Live Traffic NSW, ABC Newcastle and other publishers. NSW SES warning data is not reproduced because its direct feed needs separate authorisation; the website links to official sources instead.

Extended Weather Tools

Open-Meteo forecast and ensemble data are numerical model outputs; NOAA GFS ensemble ranges are not measured accuracy. Air quality is modelled by Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS), via Open-Meteo. Marine forecasts use modelled wave/ocean products from Open-Meteo; see marine provider attribution and documentation. Historical reanalysis is supplied through Open-Meteo using ECMWF/Copernicus datasets; see historical data sources. Regional model point samples are not continuous radar. Data is loaded on explicit request, with bounded browser caching and provider rate-limit cooldown. All third-party terms apply.

Update status

Weather API fetches occur on demand in the browser. RFS and BOM snapshots are fetched by GitHub Actions on a best-effort scheduled basis. A snapshot timestamp indicates retrieval only. If updating fails, the app should mark the source unavailable or stale.
